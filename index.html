The decision

We are starting with Odoo Community, self-hosted in a WCI-owned AWS account. It is open source with no licence cost, it is the only option we evaluated that meets every requirement without a paid tier, an unresolved vendor agreement, or a switch that cannot be undone, and it already runs the admissions pipeline in a working prototype. Patient data never sits with a CRM vendor; it stays in WCI's account under the AWS Business Associate Agreement.

Why Odoo over the alternatives, in plain terms:

  • The automations we need are built in and unlimited: timed follow-ups, stage rules, assignment, webhooks in and out, with no per-run metering.
  • CloudTalk connects natively. CloudTalk publishes its own Odoo module: click-to-call from the record, calls, notes, tags and recording links logged on the lead.
  • Nothing to buy up front. The only running cost is AWS hosting, roughly $100–300 a month.
  • It is already working. The prototype below was built on invented test data. No real patient information was used.

What is already built (prototype, test data only)

  • Your stages as they are today (Lead, Contact sequence, Potential patient, Tumor Board, Consultation, Treatment plan, Patient) and the nine closing reasons, loaded exactly as the ERP names them.
  • New inquiries are assigned automatically to the consultant with the lightest load.
  • The Day 1–4 contact sequence is created automatically as dated tasks; the Day-1 email goes out on its own.
  • A response-time clock on every inquiry, and a daily digest of overdue follow-ups to each consultant and to the Head of Admissions. This is the alert the ERP does not have today.
  • Potential-patient document checklist, Tumor Board hand-off with Candidate / More-studies buttons, a seven-day follow-up after the treatment plan, and an escalation if a case goes quiet for two weeks.
  • Not-a-Candidate letter generated from the record with one click.
  • A public "Apply to be a patient" form that creates the inquiry directly. Contact details and consent only; no medical questions on the public page.
  • Inbound and outbound webhooks, so the ERP and other systems can feed leads in and receive stage changes.
  • Supervision dashboard: leads by source and outcome, closing reasons, overdue follow-ups.
  • Full change and access logging on patient records, and the CloudTalk module installed and ready to connect.

What we need from WCI

Two items decide the compliance picture and come before anything is purchased or migrated:

  1. CloudTalk's signed Business Associate Agreement: the executed text, which plan it requires, and whether the AI Analytics add-on is covered by it. If CloudTalk will not sign one, telephony has to change regardless of which CRM we use.
  2. Counsel's view on the admissions records: confirmation of how WCI treats lead and inquiry records under HIPAA, so the controls we configure match the obligation.
  1. An AWS account owned by WCI for the CRM, with the AWS Business Associate Agreement accepted (a free, self-serve step in AWS Artifact). We can set this up with you.
  2. ERP access: the read-only server login we were given no longer works, so a fresh one please; plus an external API key issued by an ERP administrator and an OK to run a read-only test against /api/external/patients and /timeline.
  3. From Daniel: how the Meta lead automation writes into the ERP; the shape of the public /patients/public endpoint; whether the ERP can send a webhook when a patient record is created or changes stage; and moving the ERP off its development server onto a production build behind nginx, running as a non-root user.
  4. Google Workspace: confirm the Business Associate Agreement has been accepted in the Admin console.
  5. Sender domain: permission to verify a sending domain in AWS SES for the sequence emails.

The first thirty days

  1. Week 1–2: the access items above; AWS account and BAA; the prototype deployed into WCI's account, still on test data.
  2. Week 2–4: parallel run. Odoo mirrors the ERP's leads through the API while Admissions keeps working in the ERP; we tune stages, sequences and the digest with the Head of Admissions.
  3. Week 4 onward: two consultants work new inquiries in Odoo; everyone else follows once the pilot holds.
